What is MySignet?

Your keys, your faces, your people. Kept with you.

Today your logins belong to platforms, your identity is scattered across their accounts, proving you're a real adult means handing over documents, and your family's accounts are run by companies.

MySignet puts all of that back in your hands. One key you hold, a few faces you choose, a contact book of people you've confirmed, and sign-ins to sites, games and apps with no password, no email address and no account.

The problem, and the answer

The internet asks you to hand yourself over

Accounts, passwords, uploaded documents, family settings on someone else's servers. MySignet is built on Signet, an open protocol for identity, so the answer lives with you instead.

How it fits together

Three homes for three things

Your phone is where you act. A Heartwood guards your keys. A Keep looks after your things. Only the phone is needed to start; the other two are there when you want them.

Everyone

Your phone

where you act

MySignet holds your faces and your contacts, approves your sign-ins and looks after your family. On its own it's a complete, secure setup: your keys are encrypted on the device and open with your fingerprint, face or PIN.

Gold standard

A Heartwood

guards your keys

A small open-source hardware signer. Your master secret is made on the device and stays there. Your phone asks, the device signs, and only public keys come back. If the phone is lost, the key isn't.

Early, optional

A Keep

looks after your things

A small device at home that looks after your things, so they live with you rather than on someone else's cloud. Your personas own it, and only people you invite can use it.

Four ideas to know

Persona

Your everyday face. The name you choose when you start. It never carries your legal name, and at most it can show an age range. It's the default for everything, sign-ins included.

Real identity

Asleep until you need it. Created alongside your persona but switched off until you activate it. You only need it for verification, venue entry, documents and professional features. Family doesn't need it.

Recovery words

12345678910111213141516171819

19 words, in order, on paper. Words 1 to 7 are a format header, so they look much the same on every backup. Words 8 to 19 are your key. They bring everything back if you lose a phone.

Keys stay with you

No server. No account. Everything is encrypted on your device. Backups to your other devices are encrypted before they leave. MySignet shows each face's public address (npub), so you can always see which one will sign.

Choose your first step

Start where your keys will live

The app walks you through its own setup. The one choice worth making first is where your keys are born. If a Heartwood is part of your plans, start there rather than half-committing on the phone first.

A

Yes, I have one

Start on your Heartwood

Set it up first with Sapwood in Chrome or Edge, over USB. The Heartwood makes your key and shows your recovery words on its own screen, so they never pass through your phone.

Then, in MySignet, choose I already have a Signet and connect it. New personas and dependants are made on the device from then on.

Your master secret is born on the device and never touches a phone.

B

Ordered, or planning to

Wait, and start there

If a Heartwood is on its way, it's worth the wait. Starting on the phone and moving later does work, but by then your recovery words will already have lived on a phone.

Already started on the phone? That's fine too: the move in C keeps you the same person.

When it arrives, start on it, as in A.

C

No, or not sure

Start with the app

A complete, secure setup on its own. It isn't a trial or a lesser tier. Tap Create my Signet and you'll be asked for a persona name and a lock. Then write your 19 words on paper.

Move to a Heartwood later without changing who you are. The move rebuilds your keys on the device from your words, and checks every one matches before it clears them from the phone. If any key doesn't match, nothing is removed.

Your public identity, contacts and sign-ins carry on as before.

Heartwood is in beta. It's open-source firmware for small ESP32 boards such as the Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 (V3 or V4), and right now it suits people who are happy to flash firmware onto a board. Source and boards.

One honest trade-off. Two things need a key held on the phone, so they aren't available in hardware mode: adding a family member with a shared secret, and the in-person ceremony for verifying someone else.

A Keep is optional. Add one any time. It doesn't change where you start.

What you do with it

Everyday life, on your own terms

Five things MySignet is for. The app shows you how each one works when you get there.

Your contact book

A contact book that knows it's them

The people you know, alongside their keys, so you can tell the real person from a lookalike. Kept on your device and backed up, encrypted, to your other devices.

Meet in person

Scan their QR or, in the Android app, tap the phones back to back. With Bluetooth on, it can even work without the internet.

Confirm it's really them

Read a few words to each other, or check the end of their key on a call.

Bring who you already know

Any Nostr profile QR adds someone straight away, and you can import the people you already follow on Nostr.

Share what you choose

Your name and photo go with a request only if you say so. Their pictures are only downloaded when you ask.

Sign in everywhere

No password. No email. Just approve.

Where you see Sign in with Signet, MySignet does the rest. On the same phone, the app opens; on a computer or TV, you scan a QR code with your phone. Your persona is picked for you, and you approve.

Every site you've signed in to is listed in the app. Remove one and it has to ask you again. Sites and relays can still see public keys and connection metadata, which is why separate personas help keep different parts of your life apart.

Games on the big screen

Some games show a QR on the TV or computer. Approve on your phone, and your phone offers a button to open the game's own page, which can be its controller.

KithMoot

A workspace nobody owns: rooms, files and calls over Nostr, across all your devices. Sign in with Nostr, using MySignet.

Kinterest

A shared family pocket-money ledger that parent and child both see. No card, no real money. Guardians sign in with MySignet.

Nostr apps on Android

Apps such as Amethyst can ask MySignet to sign for them. You approve, and you choose the key. Your real identity is never offered while it's switched off.

Nostr apps on your computer

Turn on the Bunker and your phone signs for desktop Nostr clients you pair with it. In the Android app it can keep answering with the screen off, and tells you when something needs you.

Family

Look after the people you look after

Children get their own keys, held by you until they're ready to take them. You don't need to switch on your real identity for any of this.

Their own keys, held by you

A dependant's keys come from your own recovery words. One day they take them with them.

Their own phone

Pair their phone and check the words match on both. With a Heartwood, their phone pairs straight to it.

Asks come to you

You decide what needs your say-so. When they ask for something new, it comes to you to approve or deny. Change the rules as they grow.

My Keep

Your things, at home with you

A Keep is a small device at home that looks after your things. Your personas own it, and you choose who else gets in.

Owned by your personas

Every persona of yours can use and manage it, including ones you make later, or only the one you pick. The Keep can tell that linked personas are the same person, and the relays it uses may be able to as well. Nobody else can.

Share it with people you know

Only people you invite can play on your Keep. They get a pass, and you can revoke it; the change applies as soon as the Keep is online.

See it's online

MySignet shows the Keep you've set up at home and whether it's online.

Early days. My Keep is hidden until you want it: switch on Show My Keep in Settings → Advanced, or it appears by itself once a Keep is saved or found. It's never shown on a child's paired phone. Managing a Keep that was set up outside MySignet is coming later.

Proving things, when you need to

Wake your real identity only when it matters

It isn't used for ordinary sign-ins. You start self-declared, gain standing as people vouch for you, and can be verified by a professional rather than by any central authority. What a verifier or app learns depends on the proof you choose to share.

Activate it

Type your legal name and confirm it. From then on, that name is what verifiers, venues and your family see. A site only ever sees it if you pick it yourself.

Get verified

Visit a verifier: your GP, solicitor, teacher, accountant or another registered professional. They check your ID in person and you receive the credential on your phone.

Venue entry

Your venue entry pass is a signed QR code that refreshes every 30 seconds, so an old screenshot won't get anyone in.

Principles

What MySignet holds to, and what to expect

  • No server holds your keys. They're encrypted on your device, or kept on your Heartwood. Backups are encrypted before they leave.

  • No account. Nothing to sign up for, no password to leak, no email address to hand over.

  • Your words are the backup. Write all 19 on paper, in order, never on a screen. Lost your phone? You haven't lost you: restore on a new one with your words.

  • You choose what each site sees. Your persona by default; your real identity only if you pick it. Remove a site and it has to ask again.

Worth knowing

  • Some things are still visible. MySignet connects to relays and optional media services. Encryption doesn't hide every public event field or network connection, and sites and relays can see public keys.

  • Imported keys need their own backup. Your recovery words cover the keys MySignet made. Keep separate backups for any account you imported.

  • Android and web. Camera and biometric features depend on your device and browser. Fingerprint unlock in the Android app needs Android 11 or later; older phones use a 6-digit PIN.

  • Use your npub anywhere Nostr works. Your faces are Nostr identities, so they work across compatible apps, not just this one.

Take the right first step

Have a Heartwood, or one on its way? Start there. Otherwise, start with the app today. It's complete on its own, and it grows with you.
